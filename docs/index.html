How it works

Three gestures. No new habits.

Ledge rides on the drag you already make. There is nothing to learn and nothing to remember.

Park

Drag a window until the cursor rests against a screen edge. After a quarter second, a glowing strip shows where the tab will land. Let go.

Reveal

Click the tab. The window slides out to exactly where it was and takes focus. Click again, or just look away in Peek mode, and it slides back.

Release

Pull a tab away from the edge, or drag the revealed window off, and it becomes an ordinary window again. Nothing is ever lost.

Features

Small tabs. A lot of room.

Everything Ledge does serves one idea: your windows should be one click away, and out of the way until then.

Pop-out or Peek

Pop-out windows stay until you tuck them. Peek windows duck back when you switch apps or move the cursor away. Choose per tab, or set a default.

Hover previews

Rest on a tab to see the window behind it. With Screen Recording access the preview is live, so you can watch a download or a playing track without revealing anything.

Tab groups

Drop a window onto an existing tab to stack them. The group tab shows the apps inside and a count; click it for a list and pick the one you want.

Badges on tabs

Unread counts from the Dock show on the tab, so a parked Mail or Messages window still tells you when something arrived.

Rules per app

Keep some apps from parking at all, park others the moment they open, and teach apps like Mail to show their main window when you click them in the Dock.

Keyboard first, if you like

Park the frontmost window with ⌃⌥⌘→, ⌃⌥⌘←, or ⌃⌥⌘↓. Every shortcut can be changed.

Every display, every Space

Tabs live on the edges of any display and follow you across Spaces. They step aside while a full-screen app is in front.

Remembers your layout

Quit, restart, or log back in: the windows you parked come back as tabs, on the same edges, in the same order.

macOS 15 detail

Plays well with edge tiling

Sequoia also tiles windows dragged to the sides. Ledge can take over the left and right edges while it runs and hands the setting back when it quits.

Lineage

Pop-up windows, 1997.

Mac OS 8 let you drag a Finder window to the bottom of the screen, where it became a tab. Click the tab and the window popped up; click anywhere else and it dropped back down. People who used it never stopped missing it.

Ledge brings the idea to the Mac of today: any app, any edge, glass instead of platinum, and animations that respect Reduce Motion.

Permissions

It asks for exactly what it needs.

Accessibility

Required. This is how Ledge moves, hides, and restores windows that belong to other apps. First launch walks you through granting it.

Screen Recording

Optional. Only used for live hover previews. Without it, Ledge still works fully; previews show the app icon and window title instead.

Nothing phones home

Ledge has no account, no analytics, and no network use apart from checking for updates. Your window layout is saved in a plain file in Application Support.
